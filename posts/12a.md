## Should Inclusion Be a Structural Pillar of Creative Coding?

Yes, but inclusion shouldn't be a surface-level value tacked onto the side of creative coding. It should be woven into its infrastructure, motivations, and networks from the beginning.

### Infrastructure Shapes Possibility

Creative coding is built on digital infrastructure (frameworks, software, platforms, etc.) of communities and institutions. If those tools are only designed for people with a CS background or access to high-end tech, then we’re gatekeeping who gets to create. That’s not just about knowledge, really it’s about who has access to meaningfully participate.

Even now, many tools assume a level of digital literacy that isn’t necessarily universal, especially considering how new coding (and technology in generaly) still is. Programs are tangled in legislation or licenses that restrict how and where they can be used. Free, open-source platforms like p5.js fight against that. They’re designed to be teachable and hackable. That accessibility is what makes them powerful, it promotes learning and knowledge. When the infrastructure itself supports inclusion, it opens up space for different voices and perspectives.

### Learning: Intrinsic vs Extrinsic

People come to creative coding from wildly different places. Some are intrinsically motivated (they love puzzles or they want to express something personal), others are extrinsically motivated (maybe it's for school, a job, or a side project). When tools and communities are inclusive, they support both types of learners. They create room for experimentation.

If we gatekeep based on technical purity or computer science credentials, we squash curiosity and innovation. Computer science gatekeeping often shows up in how we dismiss people who don’t use the “right” languages, tools, or methods. Creative coding should challenge that. It should celebrate messy, hybrid, improvised ways of making.

### Power and Oppression

Large tech companies centralise platforms, data, and influence. They profit from closed ecosystems and network effects, where users get locked into one service because everyone else is using it, it's the company standard. Add in switching costs and a lack of interoperability, and it becomes hard to break out of those systems.

Inclusive creative coding offers an alternative. It imagines other ways of building, sharing, and collaborating. Ways that aren't rooted in profit or surveillance. It’s not just about making cool projects or artworks, it’s about actually about resisting the structures that limit who gets to make and how far we can push the limits.

### Hubs of Knowledge and the Critical Posthumanities

A lot of the most exciting learning doesn't happen in schools or companies. It happens in extra-institutional hubs of knowledge (like open workshops, Discord servers, collectives, zines, and community-led festivals). These spaces challenge the idea that knowledge only flows top-down.

The critical posthumanities offers another way of thinking about this. It asks us to question what counts as "human" and what counts as "knowledge." It encourages us to think beyond binaries, expert/amateur, code/art, digital/analogue. Inclusion in creative coding aligns with this. It asks us to break down who gets to participate, who gets listened to, and what counts as valuable contribution.
